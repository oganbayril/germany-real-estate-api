How accurate is it?

About half of all estimates land within — of the real asking price.

The other half miss by more, and a few miss by a lot — mostly very large, expensive or unusual flats, where there are only a handful of similar examples to learn from. Averaged over every estimate, the miss is about —.

These numbers come from a fair test: some listings are held back during training, and the model is graded on those, since it has never seen them.

What it looks at — and what it can't see

Uses

  • City
  • Neighbourhood (district, quarter, postcode area)
  • Living area in m²
  • Number of rooms
  • Floor
  • Energy efficiency class

Can't see

  • Condition or renovation
  • Year built
  • Balcony, lift, garden, parking
  • View, light, noise
  • Photos and description

So two flats that look identical on paper get the same estimate, even if one is freshly renovated and the other needs a new kitchen.

Where the data comes from

Apartments listed for sale on immowelt.de, collected automatically from the public search results a couple of times a week.

The model predicts the asking price — what the seller advertises — not what the flat actually sells for. Final sale prices are often lower.

The listings are whatever happened to be online when they were collected, not a careful sample of the whole market, and the smaller cities have far fewer examples than Berlin.

What it's for

This is a portfolio project that shows a complete machine learning system working end to end: collecting data, storing it, training a model, and serving it live on the web.

It is not a property valuation. Please don't use it to decide what to buy, sell, offer or borrow.
